\begin{abstract}
Language models can propose executable computations, but execution and agreement with a reference
answer do not prove that a computation preserves the meaning of a question.  We study partial
executable verification as feedback for inference and training in public procurement, where
questions require joins over buyer and supplier roles, complete populations, and exact aggregation.
Our system combines a graph of 215,221 contract awards that preserves provenance with learned typed
planning and deterministic grounding, execution, and release checks.  Online, a structured failure
may prompt one revised proposal without access to the answer.  Offline, positive training
candidates must also pass answer shape and hidden oracle checks.

The Qwen system with fully local inference reaches 85.73\% on 2,025 questions outside the
development subset, compared with 69.19\% for its cloud teacher and 38.42\% for retrieval
augmentation.  On the development subset containing 260 questions, supervised fine tuning is the
only transition that improves both model families, while later continuation and preference
optimisation are not uniformly beneficial.  The training harvest includes 1,262 candidates
accepted online but rejected by an offline gate, and a targeted audit finds missing constraints in
56 of 229 repair targets.  Checked execution can guide one repair attempt and training selection,
but it neither certifies semantic faithfulness nor guarantees monotonic improvement.
\end{abstract}
