\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Procurement QA with the complete runtime}

Table \ref{tab:main-results} reports the main system comparison.  The fully local Qwen pipeline is
best on the 2,025 questions outside the development subset, with 1,736 correct predictions (85.73\%).
It exceeds the cloud teacher by 16.54 points and top-40 RAG by 47.31 points.  On paired items, it
changes 372 teacher errors to correct answers and loses 37 teacher successes
($p=9.79\times10^{-71}$, exact McNemar).  The stored fully local Qwen arm differs from the stored
hybrid Qwen arm in its understander and serving stack.  Their paired predictions change 222 errors
to correct and 60 correct items to wrong ($p=5.01\times10^{-23}$).  The corresponding stored Llama
comparison also favours the fully local arm, although neither comparison isolates one component.

The non-development subset contains 1,725 answerable and 300 non-answerable questions.  Fully local
Qwen answers 1,436 answerable questions correctly (83.25\%) and withholds all 300 non-answerable
answers.  Closed-book accuracy illustrates why overall accuracy must be decomposed.  Of its 380
inclusive successes, 360 are abstentions and only 20 are correct answers.  RAG improves answerable
coverage, but the full structured runtime retains a large advantage over finite retrieval.  This
comparison evaluates complete systems and does not attribute the gap to execution alone.

\subsection{Checkpoint changes after checked execution curation}

The offline harvest first shows why execution cannot be the final training label.  Each training
question contributes one trace at question level.  Among 6,860 traces that produce an answer and pass the
online contract, 5,598 pass both offline gates.  The
other 1,262 fail the expected answer shape or typed oracle and enter the hard negative pool.  Seven
of those candidates match the oracle but fail the shape check, which is why the oracle match count
is 5,605 rather than 5,598.  Runtime checks supply a first gate, but they do not determine which
traces are eligible for positive supervision.

The development subset containing 260 questions supports paired comparisons because every checkpoint uses the same
questions and runtime.  It does not isolate one training component because the corpus and objective
change between stages, and each checkpoint has one training run.  Table \ref{tab:ladder} shows the
stored checkpoint ladder.  Supervised fine tuning is the only stage that improves both model
families.  Qwen improves by 10.77 points and Llama by 23.08 points.  The RSFT continuation
changes almost equal numbers in each direction.  Direct preference optimisation is not significant
for Qwen and is significantly worse for Llama.  Later curation stages therefore do not produce
monotonic improvement in these runs.

\subsection{Development system diagnostic}

The deterministic KG-only planner obtains 155/260 (59.62\%), naive RAG 82/260 (31.54\%), strong
RAG 75/260 (28.85\%), and the cloud teacher 192/260 (73.85\%).  The best fully local Qwen and Llama
runtimes obtain 224/260 (86.15\%) and 219/260 (84.23\%).  Qwen reaches 217/260 with the cloud Step
1 understander and the same Step 2 adapter, so 224/260 belongs to a different system configuration
rather than another planner checkpoint.  Their paired change is 13 gains and six losses
($p=0.167$, exact McNemar).

Fully local Qwen corrects 73 KG-only errors and loses four KG-only successes
($p=1.89\times10^{-17}$).  Appendix Table \ref{tab:bucket-analysis} shows the largest differences
on bridge and top-$k$ questions.  Fixed rules solve 7/20 and 1/20, while fully local Qwen solves
14/20 and 18/20.  These comparisons show coverage added by learned planning but do not isolate a
training source or runtime component.

\subsection{What verification changes and misses}

A fixed plan replay provides a narrow test of hard grounding.  Among 136 saved plans with oracles,
89 are correct before grounding and 103 after it.  Fourteen plans change from wrong to correct,
none changes from correct to wrong, and one becomes executable but remains wrong.  All 14 rescues
come from inserting the additive value guard for sums.  This is direct evidence for one runtime
intervention, not for the full collection of checks and not for a training effect.

The training target audit gives the complementary limitation.  Among 229 repair targets whose
question constraints can be mapped mechanically, 173 retain every mapped constraint and 56 are
flagged.  Of the omissions, 43 concern procurement category constraints.  A missing condition can be
redundant in the current snapshot, allowing an incomplete plan to agree with the oracle.  The audit
therefore demonstrates a blind region shared by execution and denotation based filtering.  It does
not estimate omission prevalence outside this targeted subset.  The checkpoint gains in Table
\ref{tab:ladder} also cannot be attributed specifically to grounding derived rows because the
training corpus changes task language, reader programs, ordinary plans, repairs, and abstention
examples together.

\subsection{Portability of the typed algebra}

Table \ref{tab:composition-transfer} reports the two single-call protocols.  Under the corrected
PACS manifest, Compose-v3 obtains 78.31\% on channel A and 77.11\% on channel B.  The 1.20-point
surface gap is small relative to the improvement over the base and teacher.  The result isolates
question-to-program generation; it does not include the full runtime's understanding, grounding,
evidence, or repair stages.

On WTQ, accuracy rises from 22.51\% for the base model to 27.33\% for Compose-v3, 44.43\% after
a self harvest filtered by answer agreement, and 51.80\% with translated gold programs.  All paired transitions are
significant at $p<10^{-18}$.  Because these checkpoints also differ in training data and exposure
to the output format, the comparisons do not isolate the source of each gain.
