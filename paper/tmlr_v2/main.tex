\begin{abstract}
Public-procurement data can support questions about buyers, suppliers, contract notices,
and disclosure, but many useful questions require exact set construction, multi-hop joins,
aggregation, and comparison rather than retrieval of a few relevant passages.  We present a
question-answering pipeline that maps natural-language questions to programs in a closed,
typed algebra and delegates factual authority to deterministic execution over a
provenance-preserving procurement knowledge graph.  Training programs are generated from the
data and retained only after type checking, non-degeneracy checks, and agreement between two
execution implementations.  For deployment, a structured understander is followed by either a
deterministic intent compiler or, when the intent cannot be compiled safely, a typed graph
planner; grounding, exhaustive execution, evidence checks, and bounded replanning then produce
an answer or a controlled non-answer.  On the frozen \PacsN{}-item PACS compositional test, an
8B parameter model trained with typed programs obtains \PacsOursA\% strict execution accuracy
on the canonical surface and \PacsOursB\% on an independently naturalised surface, compared
with \PacsBaseA\% for the same untuned base model and \PacsTeacherA\% for a cloud-model
planner.  On a separate balanced \RuntimeN{}-item end-to-end snapshot, the full local runtime
scores \RuntimeOurs\%, compared with \RuntimeBase\% for its untuned counterpart.  The results
support typed execution as a practical way to improve compositional reliability, while audits
also expose unresolved limitations in currency normalisation, entity aliases, benchmark
isolation, and artifact portability.
\end{abstract}
