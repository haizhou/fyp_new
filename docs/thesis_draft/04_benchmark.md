# Chapter 4 — Benchmark Construction and Split Discipline

Every empirical claim in this dissertation is only as credible as the benchmark beneath it. This chapter therefore treats the benchmark not as a fixed asset but as an *instrument with measured error*: 12,828 questions, each born from a fully parameterised gold program; oracles validated by an independent second implementation at 99.88% agreement; three abstention classes designed as traps rather than decoration; and plan-level split isolation with zero train/test plan overlap. Equally important is the honesty protocol that governs how the benchmark is *used*: the 260-question comparison set is a development set — the pipeline-v2 fixes were derived from its errors — while the 2,285-question final test set is confirmatory and is dual-reported with and without the nineteen rows that string-match development-derived cue lists. Every adaptive-overfitting risk identified in review is disclosed in this chapter, not patched over.

## 4.1 Plan-first generation: L1 templates and L2 persona rewriting

The construction principle throughout is *plan-first*: a question is never written and then annotated; instead a parameterised logical plan is instantiated against the knowledge graph, its oracle answer is computed by execution, and only then is a natural-language surface generated. Post-hoc annotation of collected questions would be more naturalistic, but its coverage is unverifiable and its cost lies outside a final-year project; plan-first generation guarantees the property this project needs most — every question carries an executable gold program, which is precisely the training target of Chapters 5–6.

**Level 1 (L1).** Thirty-three template families instantiate fully parameterised plans against the graph, execute them to obtain oracles, and verbalise canonical questions (9,044 generated; 6,772 retained after a cleaning pass removed hidden presence filters and one ill-posed conjunction family). A second, targeted generation wave (`scripts/build_targeted_v2.py`) added five deliberately harder subsets, each with control-plane validation on every run: *naturalized* (an LLM rewrite of count/sum/factoid questions, accepted only after a faithfulness gate and knowledge-graph revalidation of the oracle, with template fallback on rejection), *coverage_fixed* (answer-changing guard constraints removed and oracles recomputed), *unanswerable* (the three abstention families of §4.5, each instance schema- or graph-verified), *extended_ops* (argmax, top-k, distinct-set and two-sided comparison, each with recomputable evidence), and *bridge_join* (pure two-hop semijoins requiring decomposition). Factoid questions are anchored on a (buyer, supplier, year, CPV) tuple and admitted only if that anchor resolves to a unique answer value; count questions are readmitted only if re-execution against the graph reproduces the inherited oracle.

**Gold programs as flat specifications.** Each row's `gold_plan` is a flat, executable specification: a list of typed constraints `(field, op, value)`, an answer operation, an answer field, and machine-readable metadata. Two structural devices matter for what follows. First, multi-hop *bridge* questions are expressed by an `in_subquery` constraint whose value is a resolver payload (for example `{"resolve": "suppliers_of_buyer", "buyer": …}`); six resolver types cover the bridge inventory, so a bridge is a first-class, recomputable object rather than a prose description. Second, operations whose parameters originally lived only in the question surface were later backfilled into metadata (§4.2): grouped rankings carry `group_by`, `k` and `metric`, and every comparison carries `compare_params`. Across the whole corpus, comparison questions realise exactly four side types: paired entity counts (two independent sub-counts and a comparator), a date axis (direction plus pivot date against a signed-award date), field equality (does a categorical field equal a named value), and a numeric threshold (does an aggregate exceed a stated amount).

**Level 2 (L2).** An LLM rewrites each surface under four personas — an ordinary citizen, a policy analyst, a public-spending auditor, and an investigative journalist — with a *deterministic* per-surface accept/reject gate checking atom preservation, no invented numbers, no invented organisations, retention of unanswerability triggers, and evidence of genuine rewriting. The gate accepted 9,762 surfaces and rejected 1,170 with machine-readable reasons, dominated by invented temporal relations (890). The deterministic gate exists because the obvious alternative demonstrably fails: an LLM checker asked to validate the same rewrites approved 9,762 of 9,762, including known semantic drifts. All surfaces of a plan share one oracle, so L1-versus-L2 accuracy differences isolate language-to-plan generalisation from everything else.

**Surface diversification (v4).** A final diversification pass conditions rewrites on six explicit style axes — terse search query, verbose two-sentence context, politely embedded request, syntactic flip, multi-sentence background, and typo noise (the last confined to training data) — cycling axes deterministically, because naive LLM paraphrasing collapses toward near-copies. Acceptance is again mechanical: every gold literal must appear verbatim, no invented years or codes, a position-aware category-drift rule, and, for abstention questions, mandatory preservation of the cue words (§4.5). In the frozen benchmark, 43.8% of evaluation-split surfaces were rewritten in place with originals retained in metadata, 2,589 styled variants were added to training, and the distributional evidence is a trigram-Jaccard median of 0.429 between rewrite and source. We report this distributional evidence and claim nothing more: the surfaces are LLM-generated under controlled style axes, and no claim is made that they reproduce real-user language.

**Compositional coverage census.** To characterise what the corpus contains structurally — rather than by template *names*, which would make any novelty assertion circular — every gold plan was reduced to a structural signature: (answer operation, bridged := any `in_subquery` constraint, group-by axis, comparison side type, filter-slot combination), reconstructed programmatically from the flat specifications. Over the census scope of 12,779 rows (the four splits excluding the 49-row smoke slice), the frozen corpus realises 39 distinct (template family, answer operation) pairs, which the five-field signature refines to **38 fine-grained structural signatures collapsing to 12 coarse cells** (operation × bridged × group-by); an earlier ad-hoc count of 50 is reproduced exactly under a finer seven-field signature and is reported as a granularity variant, not corrected away. The census landmarks are themselves informative: bridging occurs only under count and sum; grouping occurs only in the non-bridged ranked-top-k family; and comparisons realise exactly the four side types above. The benchmark is thus broad at the surface level but occupies a characterisable, finite region of compositional space — a fact used in Chapter 7 when held-out compositions are probed, and an honest bound on what "coverage" means here.

## 4.2 Quality audit v1 → v2: six measured defects, six mechanical fixes

An audit of the first-generation benchmark against professional benchmark-construction practice found six defects. Each was *measured* before being fixed, each fix is mechanical, and each fix is itself testable:

1. **Boolean answers were 100% "True"** (330/330), so guessing scored perfectly. Fix: 155 mutated "False" twins (the same surface with the year moved to a zero-record year), each oracle recomputed independently; blind "True" guessing falls to 68%.
2. **Factoid answers drawn from tiny value domains** (42% of factoids) rewarded distributional guessing. Fix: 1,183 rows re-bucketed into a separate `categorical` bucket, leaving the factoid bucket with high-entropy answers.
3. **Unsupported questions were lexically detectable** (52% by a word-list probe). Fix: answerable contrast twins that swap the unsupported attribute for a supported one under otherwise identical wording, so lexical shortcuts no longer separate the classes.
4. **L2 rewrite drift was invisible to the LLM checker.** Fix: a position-aware mechanical drift detector (a category phrase appearing before the bridge relative clause modifies the outer counted noun and cannot be excused by the subquery; after it, with the subquery carrying that category, it is legitimate). Final precision on the corpus: the one true defect isolated, zero false positives.
5. **Gold programs were under-specified**: thresholds, comparison sides and ranking parameters lived only in the surface text. Fix: parameter backfill — 991 additive-money guards, 690 `compare_params` blocks, 316 answer fields, 15 top-k parameter sets — making every plan executable without reading the question.
6. **Oracle circularity**: generator and oracle shared one implementation. Fix: the independent second implementation of §4.4.

## 4.3 Splits, balance, and isolation hygiene

**Frozen arithmetic.** The frozen v4 benchmark contains **12,828 rows = train 9,267 + dev_tune 556 + dev_select 671 + dev_smoke 49 + final_test 2,285** (9,267 + 556 + 671 + 49 + 2,285 = 12,828), plus a 7,483-row surplus pool held outside all splits. The 260-question comparison set `compare_set_v4` — 13 evaluation buckets × 20 questions — is drawn entirely from within final_test; its governance is the subject of §4.6.

**How rows are assigned.** Splitting operates on *groups*, not rows (`scripts/merge_stratify_l1_l2.py`). Every row receives a near-duplicate group key hashing its template family, question type, answer operation, expected status, and the normalised entity/year/CPV/category content of its constraints; all surfaces of a plan, and all near-duplicate parameterisations, share a group, and whole groups move together, so no paraphrase of a test question can sit in training. Training is drawn first under bucket-balance constraints (a cap on any bucket's share, scarce buckets kept whole, roughly 10% of the training budget reserved for the three abstention buckets combined); development and test splits are then drawn stratified from the remainder, with the test-only ranked-top-k groups reserved directly for final_test. Three hard gates execute inside every subsequent data build: no plan may straddle the train/eval boundary, identifiers must be globally unique, and row counts must be conserved. These gates are not ceremony — an early curation bug that double-referenced 1,634 rows was caught precisely because conservation is asserted in the tooling.

**Isolation, verified from the frozen artifacts.** Train covers 6,674 distinct plan identifiers and final_test 2,184; the overlap is **zero**. The split separates *plans*, not merely surface strings: no test question shares its underlying logical program with any training question. At the family level, train carries 37 template-family labels and final_test 35, with 34 shared and one test-only family (the ranked-top-k family), retained as a small strict-holdout probe whose results belong in the limitations chapter, not the headline. One residue is disclosed here rather than hidden: an id-level deduplication missed **two verbatim train↔eval duplicate questions** (in the top-k family — identical question text under different identifiers). They are flagged and excluded from final statistics; at two rows in 2,285 their arithmetic effect is negligible, but the lesson — deduplicate on text as well as identifiers — is recorded.

**Deliberate hardness of the test set.** final_test is intentionally harder than train, because the benchmark buys statistical power where the thesis makes claims. The *hard-composite* slice — the union of L2 surfaces, the hard subsets (bridge joins, extended operations), the abstention classes, and the hard operators (compare, exists, distinct-set, argmax/argmin, ranked top-k), as implemented in `is_ood_candidate` in the split code — constitutes 54.7% of final_test against 45.0% of train, with the compositional class at 8.4% versus 1.7%; L2 surfaces are 73.3% versus 65.9%; and abstention rows are 15.8% of final_test (120 per class) versus 7.7% of train. A terminological rule is enforced throughout this dissertation: although the code field is historically named `ood_candidate`, this slice is **never described as out-of-distribution**. Both train and test contain the class; its plans are disjoint and its surfaces novel, but its *structures* are trained on. "Hard-composite" is the honest name; the only strict holdout is the single small-n test-only family. Finally, final_test flattens the bucket skew (every one of the 13 buckets has at least 93 rows; top-k has 128), so per-bucket accuracies carry comparable error bars — and dev_tune deliberately over-samples the development-era hardening targets, which is why it is never cited as an unbiased estimate.

## 4.4 Independent oracle verification: the 99.88% audit

A benchmark whose generator also produces its oracles proves nothing about correctness: agreement with the oracle is agreement with the generator. The response is a second implementation (`scripts/qa_independent_eval.py`): a pure-pandas evaluator over the raw node/edge Parquet tables that deliberately imports no code from the generator, executor, or reasoning stack, and recomputes every answerable oracle from the flat gold specifications — its own constraint application, its own six subquery resolvers, its own implementations of every answer operation.

The first pass agreed on 92.7% of oracles — and the disagreements *clustered by family*, which is exactly the diagnostic value of a second implementation: clusters localise convention gaps rather than random error. Three were diagnosed. First, the bridge-family oracles implicitly assumed the flat first-party record universe and additive-only money aggregation; a to-the-penny reconciliation of a single bridge sum (8,228,406,777.34) proved the convention, which was then made explicit in the gold programs (the 991 additive guards of §4.2) and the dataset card. Second, the comparison families' gold programs were incomplete — thresholds, pivot dates and sides lived only in the surface — motivating the `compare_params` backfill. Third, an empty-string party name was matching every record lacking that party, injecting a constant 1.61-billion excess into every bridge sum on the evaluator side; alongside it, 230 degenerate empty constraints were identified and eliminated. After these repairs, agreement is **14,752 of 14,770 answerable oracles = 99.88%**. The residual 18 are characterised, not waved away: 15 in the top-k family, where the independent evaluator must infer ranking parameters, and 3 in one boolean family evaluated by surface parsing. Five comparison-type families whose parameters are surface-borne are evaluated with declared surface-parse assistance and reported as such.

The claim licensed by this audit is deliberately bounded: 99.88% is *convention-relative agreement between two independent implementations* under the documented conventions (flat first-party universe, additive-only money, latest-release snapshot). It is not a proof of ground truth, and a different convention set would yield different oracles; it is, however, a measured answer to the question benchmarks usually leave unasked — "how do you know the oracles are right?"

## 4.5 The three abstention families as blind-region probes

The benchmark contains three abstention classes, each a first-class design object with 120 rows in final_test: **abstain_unsupported** (a real contract anchor combined with a concept the knowledge graph does not carry, such as invoice or delivery dates), **abstain_ambiguous** (a buyer anchor matching many contracts with at least three distinct values of the asked field, so no unique answer exists), and **abstain_no_results** (a real buyer paired with a CPV code it has never used, verified to match zero records). Every instance is constructed against the graph — the unsupported concept is checked against the schema, the ambiguity cardinality is counted, the empty result is executed — so abstention is never a matter of annotator opinion.

Their role in the thesis architecture (Chapter 5) explains why they are traps rather than decoration. The system's verification chain pins down every property that can be *projected* onto structure and execution; the residue — whether the plan means what the question means — is a blind region that deterministic checks cannot enter, and the abstention layer is what catches it. The three abstention families are the *completeness test of that layer*: unsupported questions probe whether the system recognises the schema boundary, ambiguous questions whether it recognises under-determination, and no-results questions whether it can distinguish a legitimate empty answer from a defect (the empty-result trap that naive repair loops fall into). Because the classes are lexically camouflaged — contrast twins remove word-list shortcuts (§4.2), and diversified rewrites must preserve the cue words, a rule added after three diversified abstention surfaces lost their cues and silently became answerable — a system can pass them only by actually performing the boundary checks. The over-sampling of these classes in final_test (15.8% versus 7.7% in train) is the benchmark buying statistical power for precisely this claim.

## 4.6 Development/confirmatory discipline and the cue-row disclosure

Midway through system development, an independent adversarial review confirmed a risk this project had created for itself. The pipeline-v2 scaffolding fixes included five new unsupported-cue strings derived directly from `compare_set_v4` misses — and four of the twenty abstain_unsupported items in that set are string-matched by them, so their v2 improvement is true *by construction* on those rows. Since `compare_set_v4` is a subset of final_test, the contamination could in principle reach the confirmatory set. Three mitigations were adopted and are binding on every results chapter:

1. **compare_set_v4 is relabelled a development set** for all post-v2 claims. Its numbers select models and diagnose failures; they are never cited as headline results. Its only role in this chapter is to define the discipline.
2. **final_test is confirmatory**: evaluated once per system generation, after all fixes are frozen.
3. **Dual reporting.** The five development-derived cue strings string-match 19 of the 2,285 final_test rows (0.8%). Every headline number is reported both including and excluding these rows:

| System | all 2,285 | excl. 19 cue rows | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| teacher | 69.76% | 69.55% | −0.21pt |
| Qwen hybrid | 78.03% | 77.89% | −0.14pt |
| Qwen fully-local | 85.65% | 85.57% | −0.08pt |
| Llama hybrid | 75.97% | 75.82% | −0.16pt |
| Llama fully-local | 83.33% | 83.23% | −0.10pt |

Every headline moves by at most 0.21 points; no ranking and no significance verdict changes. One sentence is mandatory whenever this table appears, because without it the negative deltas invite the wrong reading: **the deltas are negative because the 19 cue-matched rows were mostly answered correctly by *all* systems** — removing near-universally-correct rows lowers every accuracy slightly. The dual report bounds the adaptive-overfitting exposure; it does not reveal an advantage flowing to any one system. Together with the two disclosed verbatim duplicates (§4.3), this is the complete list of known train/eval contamination channels, each bounded and reported rather than silently repaired.

## 4.7 v4.1 curation and the evaluation-protocol footnote

One further intervention on the frozen test set is disclosed because frozen-set doctrine forbids silent edits. Objectively malformed question *text* is a data-quality defect, not a difficulty feature, so a v4.1 curation pass was run under four rules: defects are identified by system-independent text patterns only (never by "questions systems got wrong"); the sweep covers the full sets; only surface text may change — plans and oracles are untouched; and the change is versioned with a backup of the pre-edit file. The regex sweep over four malformation classes (raw dictionary literals leaked from the rewrite pipeline, unfilled placeholders, whitespace runs, duplicated-word runs) flagged four final_test rows, of which **two were fixed** (a trailing resolver-dictionary artifact stripped from a bridge question; a duplicated year collapsed in a date question) and two were adjudicated as legitimate English; `compare_set_v4` had zero hits. Integrity checks confirm 2,285 rows with identifiers and oracles byte-identical and only the two question strings changed; since nothing had yet been evaluated on final_test, no result mixes versions. The impact bound is ≤0.09 points — which also closes an argument: the difficulty of final_test is design (hard-composite share, L2 surfaces, abstention traps), not residual data noise.

A final footnote separates two configurations that must never be blended. The *evaluation protocol* used for every cited accuracy runs with a repair budget of one; the *data-engine harvest* of Chapter 6 deliberately runs with a repair budget of two, because a harvester should extract as much verified supervision as each question allows. "Data-engine yield" and "system accuracy" are therefore different metrics under different budgets, and no table in this dissertation compares a number of one kind against a number of the other.

In sum, the benchmark's authority rests on four measured properties — executable gold programs, dual-implementation oracle agreement of 99.88% with the residual 18 characterised, plan-level split isolation verified at zero overlap, and abstention traps that test the system's blind region — and on a use discipline that quarantines development feedback loops and dual-reports the one bounded exception. The chapters that follow spend these credentials; this chapter is where they were earned.
