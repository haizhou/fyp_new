# Chapter 10 — PACS: A Sealed, Necessity-Audited Primary Benchmark

Chapter 9 closed with an honest list of what its probe could not claim: eight hand-designed families, a rendering idiom shared with the training generator, synthetic surfaces throughout. If the compositional planner is to be evaluated rather than merely exercised, it needs a benchmark whose questions were *not* written by the machinery being tested, whose test split is sealed before any model sees it, and whose headline number can survive the two standard attacks on executable benchmarks — surface leakage and logical reducibility. This chapter builds that benchmark, the Procurement Analytics Challenge Set (PACS), evaluates the frozen compose-v3 planner on it exactly once, and then audits the result from both directions.

## 10.1 Design: intent clusters, orthogonal axes, sealed split

PACS is organised around *intents* — analytical questions specified as algebra trees before any surface text exists — arranged along three orthogonal axes: seven task families (existence and lookup through grouped extremes, set operations, universal quantification), three depth levels, and a seen/unseen exposure axis defined by whether the intent's logical shape appeared anywhere in the training distribution. A fourth axis carries answerability status: alongside answerable rows, every family contributes rows whose correct behaviour is refusal — ambiguous references, empty results, fields the schema does not carry, operators the grammar does not contain. Scoring for these rows is dual: *Status Exact Match* (only an explicit abstention counts) and a faithfulness-gated *Safe Semantic Outcome* (a runtime multiple-answer or empty result counts only if every literal in the emitted tree traces to the question — accidentally failing wrong plans earn nothing).

Each intent renders into up to three surface channels: channel a, an independently written surface grammar that shares no code or phrasing with the training renderer and serves as the primary channel; channel b, a locally-run LLM naturalization pass admitted only when literal-preservation and logic-preservation gates both pass (660 of 1,012 rewrites passed; 352 fell back to channel a; 179 were copied for missing-operator rows — all recorded); and channel c, the training idiom itself, retained as a diagnostic only. Isolation is enforced by a ledger of seven identifiers per row and five zero-overlap gates between train and test at the levels of exact question text, logical signature, surface grammar template, entity-anchor signature, and tree hash. The first sealing attempt failed two gates — thirty exact-question collisions through channel-c anchor coincidences, and thirty-one status variants inheriting exposure labels from their base intents — and was aborted; a sanitation pass dropped colliding clusters whole and relabelled variant exposure from each variant's own shape. The final seal: PACS-dev 231 rows over 173 clusters for diagnosis, PACS-test 922 rows over 694 clusters, sealed by checksum, evaluated under a one-confirmatory-run-per-frozen-config rule.

A 93-row human audit sample was read before unsealing results. It surfaced one real defect: the empty-result variant search had treated boolean `False` as "empty", mislabelling 46 rows (10 dev, 36 test) that in fact had answers. The v1.1 correction relabelled them and re-executed the stored model trees offline against corrected oracles — zero new model calls — and the headline *rose*: the model had answered 35 of the 36 test rows correctly all along.

## 10.2 The confirmatory run and its references

On PACS-test channel a, frozen compose-v3 under grammar-constrained decoding, one call per question: **78.31% strict overall; answerable 80.00% (584/730), intent-cluster bootstrap 95% CI [77.06, 82.92]; status rows 71.9% Status Exact Match and 91.7% Safe Semantic Outcome**. The unseen-exposure gap is 11.5 points [5.6, 17.2] — real, bounded, and consistent with Chapter 9's conditional-extrapolation picture. Family scores range from 96–99% (existence, lookup, aggregation) through 70–94% (set operations, grouped comparison) down to 39–41% for the two families that were weakest in dev diagnosis as well: scope-binding under multiple filters and universal quantification on novel surfaces. Naturalization costs −2.37 points paired on the genuinely-rewritten subset — wording matters, but does not collapse the result. Inference cost is one call, median 1.15 s, ≈1.3k tokens.

Three same-set references calibrate the number. The untrained base model under identical decoding scores 36.01% strict. A lexical-retrieval RAG baseline with a local 8B reader reaches 21.90% on the answerable subset. And the cloud teacher — grok-4-1-fast, free decoding, because constrained decoding is unavailable through the API — scores **50.33% strict / 56.62% safe: the distilled 8B student exceeds its teacher by 27.98 points on the primary benchmark**, with 23.1% of teacher rows lost to format failures (156 invalid trees, 57 unparseable). The asymmetry is disclosed rather than removed; it is also the deployment argument, since the format channel that guided decoding closes is available only when you control the server.

## 10.3 Auditing the headline: reducibility and repair

Two audits attack the result from opposite sides. The first asks whether the benchmark is too easy in a specific, fashionable sense: recent complex-query-answering work shows many multi-condition questions are answerable from a sub-query, so a high headline may reward partial programs. A constraint-necessity audit deletes each oracle-tree predicate in turn and re-executes: on the test split, 58.5% of predicates are individually necessary and 42.7% of answerable rows are fully irreducible — every predicate load-bearing. On that irreducible, shortcut-resistant subset the planner scores **78.7%, within 1–3 points of its 80.0% answerable headline**. The headline is not inflated by reducible rows.

The second audit asks the mirror question: is the single-call protocol underselling the system? A typed-feedback repair loop — oracle-blind, triggered only by hard failures (parse, type-check, malformed-plan execution errors), with abstentions, answers, and semantically meaningful empty or multi-answer outcomes declared final and never reflected — was evaluated on PACS-dev: +1.73 strict / +2.17 safe, four rows fixed, zero broken, abstention families untouched, at a cost of nine reflections in 231 rows. The design encodes a lesson the main pipeline learned the hard way in Chapter 6: an earlier reflector that repaired on "no answer" alone converted correct abstentions into hallucinations and cost eighteen points. Whether a reflect configuration should spend the sealed test's one-run budget is left as an explicit open decision, not taken by default.

## 10.4 Relation to the legacy system

On the frozen legacy test set (2,285 items, per-item paired), the compositional planner reaches **87.38% on answerable questions against the legacy champion's 83.01%** (+237/−153 discordants, McNemar p = 2.5e-05) — a single call, no repair, against a two-stage pipeline with a repair budget. On the 360 abstention rows the strict metric collapses to 13.9%: the planner answers where the legacy abstention machinery refuses, and the safe metric recovers 181 of those rows as faithful empty results while denying credit to 58 unfaithful accidental failures. Overall 75.80% strict / 83.72% safe against 85.65% (safe-metric difference not significant, p = 0.059). Under the pre-registered retirement bar — no key-bucket regression — **no retirement claim is made**: refusal is a key bucket, the compose training mix carried only 37 abstention demonstrations, and the boundary itself is the finding. Answerable competence transfers to a single cheaper call; refusal behaviour does not come for free.
