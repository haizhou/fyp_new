# Chapter 9 — Opening the Composition Space: Teaching a Local Planner to Compose

The dissertation so far treated the executable plan language as fixed. Chapter 7's compositional pilot located the consequence: the executor supported novel bridge compositions but rejected the two non-bridge aggregation compositions chosen for the probe, and the boundary was the pipeline's, not the learner's. This chapter asks the question that finding raises. If the plan language itself is opened — from a closed whitelist of operator patterns to a small recursive algebra in which operators compose freely — can an 8-billion-parameter student learn to *use* that freedom? The answer, established through three training iterations and an adversarial check battery, is yes, with a precisely mapped boundary: composition over demonstrated constructs is learned essentially to ceiling and survives wording changes; construction-level extrapolation is real but conditional; and every fix to the training distribution narrowed behaviour somewhere else, which only the check battery — never the headline number — caught.

All work in this chapter is a parallel experimental track. The frozen v2.2 pipeline and every result of Chapters 5–7 are untouched. Supervision in this track is a different regime from the main ladder's and we state it once here: training examples are *program-authored* — a generator writes both the plan and its question — and *dual-verified*, with two independent evaluator implementations recomputing every answer and any disagreement discarding the row. The main ladder's filter-never-author discipline concerned learning from unverified model outputs; this track's questions never pass through a model at all. No teacher API call and no human label appears anywhere in the training loop.

## 9.1 A grammar-closed, composition-open plan algebra

The v2.2 executor consumes a fixed specification shape: a flat list of AND-ed constraints, exactly one answer operation from a ten-element whitelist, and parameters in metadata. Multi-step forms exist only as hardwired patterns (the `in`-semijoin chain and a two-count comparison), so the pilot's failures were failures by construction: "group by year, then take the extremum" is not a sentence the language can say.

The replacement is a typed recursive algebra of seventeen node types. Leaves filter the flat record universe (predicates now include negation, disjunction, and set-membership against a computed subtree — the semijoin generalised and given an anti-join twin). Interior nodes project value sets, aggregate (count, sum, extremum-record), group (key plus count/sum metric), reduce groups (extremum key, top-k), combine numbers (comparisons, difference, ratio), combine value sets (union, intersection, difference), and combine group tables key-wise. A tree is validated by a recursive type checker whose failures are typed diagnostics, mirroring the verifier discipline of Chapter 5; depth and size are capped. The grammar is closed — every node type and enum is fixed — but the space of trees is combinatorially open. This is the design point of the whole chapter: the verifiability that Chapter 5 obtained from closed enumerations survives, because verification moved from "is this operator on the whitelist" to "is this tree well-typed under the grammar", which is exactly as mechanical.

Two independent evaluators execute trees. The runtime evaluator shares the knowledge-graph loading path and constraint semantics with the production system; the independent evaluator rebuilds the record universe from the raw node and edge Parquet files with no shared code, following the dual-implementation discipline of Chapter 4. Both enforce the frozen conventions: deduplication on the contract identifier, exclusion of empty group keys, exact decimal money arithmetic, deterministic tie-breaks.

Before anything was trained, the new machinery had to pass the gate the user set for it: *the new evaluation layer must reproduce the old questions.* A deterministic converter translates every frozen gold plan of the Chapter 4 benchmark into an algebra tree. On the final test set, both evaluators reproduce the frozen oracles at **100.00%** of the covered scope (1,823/1,823 each; the 102 rows of one comparison family whose target value exists only in the question surface are excluded, as in the original dual-oracle audit). On the training split the figure is 99.98%, and the two residual rows are a malformed training question whose gold plan had lost its CPV constraint — a defect of the benchmark, not the evaluator, recorded and left unrepaired. The translation exercise found three small benchmark defects (a k-parameter contradiction in seven top-k rows, row-order tie-breaks in six early oracles, and the malformed pair), each handled by a declared convention rather than silently.

## 9.2 The compose probe: novel compositions with dual-verified answers

To measure anything, we need questions the old system could not express, with trustworthy answers. The compose probe contains 324 rows in eight families arranged by compositional distance from the old training distribution, plus controls. Near: grouped temporal argmax (N1) and per-side filtered sum comparison (N2) — the two pilot failures. Middle: count ratios (C1a), sum differences (C1b), and grouped comparison (C5). Far: supplier set difference (C2), universal quantification via relational division (C3), and cross-role union counting (C4). Out-of-grammar: median and monotonic-trend questions (P1/P2), for which no valid tree exists and the correct behaviour is abstention.

Every answerable row is born as a tree, evaluated by both implementations, and discarded on any disagreement; degenerate instances (empty scopes, knife-edge comparisons, oversized answer sets) are filtered by per-family sensibility gates. Two construction notes record honest contact with the data: the originally planned universal property for C3 (procurement category) turned out to be a deterministic function of the CPV division in this corpus — every division-year slice is single-category — so the quantifier would have been vacuous; the property was changed to a per-notice disclosure flag that genuinely varies (mixed in 195 of 224 slices). And C5 anchors exclude comparison years outside the corpus, where the second side is empty and the question degenerates.

## 9.3 Zero-shot emission: the lock-in result and its reversal

The first experiment asked what the *existing* models do with the open grammar, given only a prompt describing it: identical prompt (grammar, field catalogue, two format-only examples using old-style single operations), one call, temperature zero, no repair, and — deliberately — no guided decoding, so that local and API systems face symmetric conditions.

Under this free-decoding protocol the six arms separate dramatically: the cloud teacher reaches 62.96% (64.81% after an evaluator fix described below), the untrained Qwen3-8B base 32.41%, and the fine-tuned students collapse — Qwen SFT 2.47%, Qwen DPO 0.00%, Llama base 0.00%, Llama SFT 0.31%. Read alone, this is a striking dose–response: the more the student was tuned on the old flat contract, the less it can emit in the new recursive one, while its own untouched base retains a third of the task. We initially read it as capability destruction.

The reading did not survive the instrument check. A recursive JSON schema for the algebra turns out to be enforceable by constrained decoding in the serving stack, and under guided decoding — the configuration every production number in this dissertation already uses — the picture inverts: the DPO student recovers from 0.00% to **45.06%** and the base rises to 49.38%. The collapse was a *format-channel* phenomenon. Raw-output autopsies show the mechanism: the tuned students emit semantically near-correct trees with misplaced node attributes and unclosed brackets (their training contract was flat, and a lifetime under guided decoding never exercised bracket discipline), plus occasional old-vocabulary intrusions. A mechanical bracket-repair pass rescued 224 of 272 malformed outputs into parseable JSON and exactly zero into correct answers — attribute misplacement is unrepairable after the fact — which is why only in-decode constraint could separate format from semantics. The methodological lesson is recorded alongside the result: post-hoc repair and in-decode constraint are not interchangeable instruments.

Two evaluator sharp edges surfaced here and were fixed as a declared convention change (evaluator v1.1): numeric-string literals in range predicates — a defensible model choice, since CPV codes are string-typed everywhere else — previously crashed comparison, biting the teacher (14 rows) and the students alike. Re-scoring stored trees offline moved the teacher from 62.96% to 64.81% and the compose-track adapters by at most 0.3 points; every affected number in this chapter is reported under v1.1.

The zero-shot experiment leaves a clean starting map: with format externally enforced, both base and tuned models hold 45–49% on novel compositions; universal quantification (C3) is at zero for every arm under every protocol; and the out-of-grammar controls are mostly answered rather than refused. That is the gap the training track must close.

## 9.4 Teaching composition: program-authored, dual-verified SFT

The data engine samples type-correct trees from compositional recipes — random filter scopes (one to three predicates, with random negation and disjunction decorations) wrapped by operation patterns that nest freely, including set operations over two scopes, group-wise comparisons, semijoin and anti-join bindings, and a nested bind-then-aggregate form. Each node renders an English clause, so the question is composed exactly as the tree is. Both evaluators execute every candidate; disagreement or a failed sensibility gate discards it. Every kept row carries a *shape signature*: the tree's skeleton with node labels and predicate operators, literals stripped.

The first pool held 8,000 rows across 686 distinct shapes. The training split is cut at the shape level — whole shapes move together, and a fifth of shapes, plus one designated construction, are held out entirely as the B set. Three ingredients join the synthetic rows: 3,631 old-benchmark questions translated into the algebra by the regression-validated converter (capped at 150 per family, so the unified adapter keeps the old competence — the user's requirement that the new model must not forget the old questions); 37 abstention demonstrations for out-of-grammar templates; nothing else. Training is deliberately light and always from the untouched base — never from the old flat-contract adapters, whose format prior is the documented lock-in — two epochs of the same QLoRA shape as the main ladder, roughly two GPU-hours.

The first adapter (compose-v1) was judged on four axes under the production-realistic guided protocol, and then subjected to a check battery proposed in review. The headline: held-out shapes 92.66% against the base's 44.51% on identical questions. The checks then did their work. A leakage audit found the exporter had asserted shape-disjointness only within the synthetic pool, while the old-task translation channel re-introduced 196 easy shapes into training; the leak-free recut — **B_clean, 1,534 rows: 91.72% versus base 38.98%, discordant pairs +814/−5** — is the number this chapter treats as v1's result. An evaluator audit re-scored 300 of the model's emitted trees with the independent implementation: 300/300 verdict agreement, no executor-side bias. A masked negative control (entities and years replaced by placeholders) crashed accuracy to 6% as it must. The strict construction holdout — the grouped-comparison form, verified absent from all 9,938 training rows at the node level — scored 64.8% against the base's 6.1%: genuine extrapolation to an undemonstrated construction.

One check found the weakness. Meaning-preserving *clause reordering* — the same question with its scope clauses permuted — dropped v1 from 97.9% to 53.2% on the affected sample, while two wording paraphrases (verbose and stem-substituted) held at 96% and 94.7%. The model had learned composition, and had also anchored part of it to the fixed clause order of the training renderer. (The first version of this perturbation was itself buggy — its reordering crossed comparison boundaries and changed meanings in 56 rows — and was caught and repaired before any conclusion was drawn; the instrument audit habit of Chapter 7 pays for itself here.)

## 9.5 The boundary experiment: why the two hard constructions failed

Two probe families stayed near zero for v1: universal quantification (C3, 0/32) and the probe's phrasing of grouped comparison (C5, 2/40) — despite the 64.8% on the B set's grouped comparisons. A factorial boundary experiment separated the candidate causes. Rewriting the probe questions into the training surface style changed nothing (C5: 1/40) — the surface hypothesis fails. Placing *two worked examples* of the constructions in the prompt took both families to **100%** (32/32 and 40/40), with or without the rewrite. The entire failure was demonstration absence, and the apparent contradiction with the B-set result dissolves on inspection of content rather than wording: the probe's grouped comparisons share one anchor and differ only by year ("more X in 2024 than in 2023"), and the model resolves this comparative ellipsis by reading the second year as a numeric threshold — `keys_where(…, op=gt, value=2023)` — a mistake the B set's structurally different two-scope comparisons never invite. C3's autopsy is equally specific: after the type-coercion fix its trees execute, but they intersect the wrong sets; the "all of whose" construction — everyone minus counterexamples — was simply never seen.

A sampling probe closes the section: at temperature 1.0 with sixteen samples under guided decoding, the trained model finds a correct C3 tree for 3 of 32 questions and a correct C5 tree for 13 of 40. Exploration can reach both constructions, so reinforcement learning with the dual-verified reward (whose hacking surface is closed by construction: every training question carries an independently agreed answer) is viable in principle. It is also, on this evidence, unnecessary for coverage: two demonstrations already suffice. We therefore kept reinforcement learning shelved as a methodological question — whether exploration can *replace* demonstration — rather than a needed fix, and repaired the gap in the data instead.

## 9.6 Iteration v2: order randomisation and ordinary recipes

The second iteration made three data-level changes and no architectural ones. The question renderer shuffles clause order per row, attacking the order anchor. The grouped-comparison recipe gained the same-anchor elliptical variant that the probe exposed. Universal quantification became an ordinary sixteenth recipe at its natural sampling share — deliberately *not* a targeted oversample, after the user's (correct) challenge that special-casing the failing families risks the very overfitting this track is meant to avoid. The strict construction holdout rotated to set intersection: union and difference are demonstrated, intersection never, testing within-family construction transfer. The leakage audit now runs before training, and B_clean (1,187 rows after excluding 182 old-task-channel collisions) was fixed before any evaluation.

compose-v2's judgment, all under the guided protocol:

| evaluation | compose-v2 | reference |
|---|---|---|
| B_clean (1,187 unseen shapes, pre-audited) | **98.99%** | base 46.84%; discordants **+619/−0** |
| set-intersection construction holdout | **39/39** | base 21/39 |
| compose probe (324) | **88.58%** | v1 adapter 78.40% |
| — C3 universal quantification | **32/32** | was 0/32 everywhere |
| — C5 grouped comparison | **40/40** | was 2/40 |
| old-task retention (400-row final-test sample) | **87.5%** | v1 87.0% |
| clause reorder perturbation | 98.0 → **74.0** | v1: 97.9 → 53.2 |
| wording paraphrases (verbose / stem) | 98.0 / 99.3 | robust |
| masked negative control | 10.7% | crashes, as required |

Three readings. First, the intersection holdout is the chapter's sharpest single cell: a construction never present in training, solved perfectly by composing its demonstrated siblings, against a base that manages half. With +619/−0 discordants on B_clean, the claim "composition over the demonstrated grammar is learned" no longer rests on any aggregate. Second, the order-sensitivity gap halved (44.7 to 24.0 points) but did not close; per-row random orders teach invariance only statistically. Third — the honest ledger — the fix narrowed behaviour elsewhere: cross-role union (C4), which v1 answered 40/40 by inheriting the base model's generalisation, fell to 7/40, because "as a buyer or as a supplier" appears in no recipe surface and v2's tighter fit displaced the inherited behaviour (the model now silently drops the second role). One abstention control also slipped (P2, 6/6 to 4/6). Neither regression is visible in any headline; both were caught by the battery. We state the general lesson as this track's second methodological finding: *in synthetic-data iteration, every rendering fix narrows the distribution somewhere else, and a standing perturbation battery — not the headline metric — is the instrument that detects it.*

A third iteration (v3: a cross-role union recipe variant and paired order-twin surfaces for the same tree) was launched to close the two known gaps; its results update this chapter's table if they arrive before the freeze, and change nothing about the claims above if they do not.

## 9.7 What this means for the thesis

The one-sentence thesis said supervision flows only from the verifiable region while competence extends beyond it. This chapter stress-tests the sentence at the level of the *language* rather than the data distribution, and both of its clauses sharpen.

The first clause strengthens: in the open algebra, the verifiable region is the entire grammar. Program-authored generation with dual-implementation verification makes supervision fully verified at zero marginal cost — no teacher, no labels, roughly two GPU-hours per iteration — and the resulting 8B adapter composes at 99% on unseen shapes while retaining the old task at 87.5%. Partial verifiability was enough in Chapters 5–7; total verifiability, where obtainable, is better, and the recursive-grammar design shows it can be obtained without giving up the closed-enumeration checkability that made the original verifier strong.

The second clause acquires structure. Competence extends beyond the demonstrated region — the intersection holdout and the 64.8% keys-where extrapolation are unambiguous — but not uniformly: it fails exactly where a construction has never been seen *and* the question's content pattern invites a wrong reading (comparative ellipsis, universal quantification), and it recovers completely from two demonstrations. Meanwhile the lock-in experiment adds a mirror image to Chapter 7's schema diagnostic: fine-tuning taught contract conformance so deeply that, decoded freely, it overwrites the base model's latent ability to emit any other contract — yet the ability survives underneath, recoverable the moment decoding is constrained. Both results say the same thing from opposite sides: what looks like capability in these systems is often the composition of a base competence with a format channel, and instruments that cannot separate the two will misread both.

## 9.8 Limitations

The B set is generated by the same recipe engine as the training data; unseen *shapes* are unseen, but the rendering idiom is shared, and the reorder result shows idiom matters. The probe's eight families are hand-designed, not sampled from users. All question surfaces are synthetic English; nothing here measures real-user phrasing. The residual 24-point order gap and the C4 regression are open defects at the time of writing (v3 targets both). The guided-decoding dependence is documented rather than removed: free-decoding numbers are reported, and they are much worse for every tuned model. Reinforcement learning from the dual-verified reward remains unrun — deliberately, as the boundary experiment showed demonstrations dominate it for coverage — so the question of whether exploration can replace demonstration stays open. Finally, this track inherits the single-domain scope of the dissertation: one knowledge graph, one schema, one corpus.
